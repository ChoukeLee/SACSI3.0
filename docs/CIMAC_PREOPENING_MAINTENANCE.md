# CIMAC pre-opening maintenance

This controlled API is for explicitly authorized corrections of **unstarted CIMAC shop reservations**. It does not execute a bank transfer, start or renew a lease, create rental arrears, infer an exchange rate, or allow arbitrary SQL/table patches.

## Authorization and request identity

- The current normal-account bearer session is verified with Supabase Auth. There is no browser-cookie fallback or service-role client.
- Only an administrator who can access CIMAC may use the named maintenance RPCs. Anonymous, service-role, and direct private-table access remain denied.
- Connector 0.8.0 introduces `query_cimac_maintenance`, `execute_cimac_maintenance`, and `cimac_maintenance_status`. Older native USD/CNY rent collections retain their 0.7.0 wire contract.
- Keep the same UUID for the entire batch, including an unknown result or retry. A previously completed request returns `completed_previously` with `verified=false`; it is not a fresh reconciliation.
- The query/prepare stages are read-only. Execution binds the exact request, authenticated actor, whole-scope snapshot, expiry, and deployment to a signed preview.

## Bounded operations

The request contains at most 30 typed operations: `terms`, `close`, `replace`, `reserve`, `area`, `create_reservation`, or `note_receipt`. Their field schemas are defined in `src/features/business-actions/cimac-maintenance.ts`; unknown fields and ambiguous targets fail closed.

- **Terms** correct per-contract XOF monthly rent and three-month deposit, the confirmed free-rent period, and area-based property-fee rules. Existing receipts are preserved; the paid-deposit flag is recomputed from known historical financial evidence.
- **Close** records a confirmed full deposit refund against exact original payment IDs and cancels only the unstarted reservation. A refund date is not used as a checkout/lease-end date. A verified note-only receipt may be formalized first, once for the agreement rather than once per shop.
- **Replace** preserves the former customer, original receipt, refund, and historical move chain, then creates an independent new customer/reservation and confirmed XOF deposit receipt. It never transfers the former customer's money to the new customer.
- **Reserve** updates grouped shop holdings without inventing a per-shop split of a confirmed group price. It configures the confirmed property-fee policy but creates neither a signed lease nor a receipt.
- **Area/create reservation** use explicitly confirmed physical shops and areas. New reservations have no invented signing, commencement, or end dates, and no receipts.
- **Note receipt** requires an exact dated original CNY deposit token and source-note match; partial numeric substrings do not prove an amount. It links the old registration to a single formal historical receipt.

## Money and reporting

Amounts are original-currency decimal strings; XOF amounts are whole francs. In this maintenance contract, CNY payments/refunds are bank transfers and new XOF deposit receipts are cash. Other methods require a separately reviewed extension, not a guessed value.

Converted historical CNY deposits are refunded in CNY with their original immutable FX valuation. Previously unvalued CNY note receipts/refunds use `native_settlement=true`, null FX, zero XOF ledger valuation, and explicit native amounts. They remain outside XOF totals and rental income. Native deposits/refunds appear as liabilities, and CIMAC CSV exports identify them as unconverted original-currency amounts.

System financial references are generated by the existing numbering trigger. Physical receipt numbers do not control financial numbering. New maintenance payments and their ledger evidence cannot be edited/deleted through legacy direct table grants.

## Transaction and audit guarantees

The database rechecks admin/project access and exact preconditions, takes deterministic row/advisory locks, and executes the whole batch atomically. Snapshot drift, partial refunds, inconsistent old ledger evidence, duplicate receipts, wrong customers, active leases, unauthorized projects, or unknown fields roll back the batch.

Private default-deny request records contain the exact request, actor, before/after snapshots, deployment and result. The ordinary audit log records the batch and source. The HTTP endpoint and connector validate per-payment/ledger evidence rather than trusting a bare success flag. Original payments, original ledger entries, and receivables must remain unchanged apart from the explicitly added receipts/refunds; no receivables are created.

The warehouse and shop 231 are deliberately excluded from this initial maintenance surface. Current holdings must not be confused with a retired customer's historical financial records.

## Release and operation

1. Preserve scoped business and schema/ACL backups; inspect security advisories.
2. Run parser/HTTP/connector tests, full application validation, clean schema rebuild and native PostgreSQL regression tests.
3. Apply the reviewed schema-only migration; verify no business data changed and no new unexpected permissions/security warnings appeared.
4. Commit/push, verify CI and the production deployment commit is READY.
5. Package the reviewed connector, verify the normal logged-in actor and read-only preview, then execute the explicitly approved batch once under its saved request ID.
6. Independently reconcile all affected records and exclusions. Preserve the request and proof; never re-send a new UUID merely because the response was lost.

Connector pending records are DPAPI-encrypted, versioned, and recovered by querying the original request; recovery never replays a maintenance write automatically.
